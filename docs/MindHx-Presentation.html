01 / Objective

Lower the barrier
to a first screening step.

Depression, anxiety, and related conditions are frequently under-screened, especially where access to mental-health professionals is limited and stigma discourages self-report. MindHx's objective is a private, low-friction check-in that surfaces a risk signal a person can act on - explicit about what it is not: not a diagnosis, not a replacement for a clinician, not a clinically calibrated instrument (yet).

Multimodal, not single-signal

Voice, text, and three validated questionnaires are fused into one explainable score with a genuine per-signal attribution breakdown.

Route, don't diagnose

Risk-tier language throughout, never diagnostic language. Crisis signals short-circuit everything and go straight to Emergency Support.

Minimal data, accounts optional

The core check-in never requires an account. Audio is processed and discarded. Only score/band/themes are ever saved.

01 / Introduction — Why MindHx Matters

MindHx matters because mental health screening is still hard and expensive to access, especially in a country like Pakistan where psychiatrists are scarce and social stigma keeps people from speaking up.

It combines three signals at once - how someone sounds, what they say, and validated PHQ-9, GAD-7, K10 questionnaires - into one clear score with a breakdown of what contributed to it.

Rather than a formal diagnosis, it assesses the severity of the situation and underlying risk factors by detecting themes like grief, trauma, suicide risk, anxiety, depression, and pain, and uses this to route elevated risk straight to a professional. If something like self-harm comes up, it redirects to emergency support immediately, before any score is even shown.

Signing in is optional, only for people who want their check-in history saved, and even then only the score and general themes are stored, never a transcript or written answers. Audio itself is always deleted right after processing. It also works fully in Urdu and English, making it usable locally rather than just another English-only app.

02 / Why This Matters for Pakistan

A screening gap that is
structural, not incidental.

  • Access: Pakistan has one of the world's lowest psychiatrist-to-population ratios - widely cited estimates put it under 500 psychiatrists for a population of 240+ million, concentrated in a handful of major cities.
  • Stigma: seeking mental-health help is often read as personal weakness or family shame, especially outside urban centers, which keeps people from taking even a first, private step.
  • Language: Urdu is the national language and lingua franca, yet most digital mental-health tools are English-only - quietly excluding the people most likely to need them.
  • Cost and distance: a first appointment often requires travel, time off work, and a fee just to find out whether something is worth pursuing further.

MindHx is built directly against these four barriers: private and free to try, no account required, fully bilingual, and paired with a real, verified directory of Pakistani providers for the moment a person is ready for a real conversation.

03 / The Product

How the combined score is calculated

Each signal - acoustic, linguistic, and clinical - is scored independently first, then combined into one weighted estimate using an additive model. Each term's share in the final number is the real, mathematically exact contribution, not an approximation bolted on afterward - shown directly on the results page.

04 / The AI Chatbot — Everyday Support

A middle ground between doing nothing and seeing a professional

MindHx also includes an AI chatbot built for everyday stress, anxiety, and burnout - the kind of thing most people never bring up with anyone. It only answers from a fixed, reviewed set of information, so there is no risk of unsafe advice.

Most people here never see a professional for smaller issues like poor sleep, work pressure, or daily anxiety. Through the chatbot they get simple meditation techniques and basic therapy approaches right away - no appointment, no waiting.

It fills a middle ground. Not a replacement for real treatment, but not leaving someone with nothing either. If the conversation turns serious, it stops and redirects to emergency support instead of continuing.

05 / Urdu Language — Technical Safety

Defense in depth:
never show an English reply
when Urdu was requested.

The AI's system prompt instructs Qwen to reply in the requested language - but LLMs don't always comply. MindHx adds a server-side guard, is_urdu_script(), that checks the letter composition of any generated reply. If Urdu was requested and the reply reads as English, it is discarded and replaced with the guaranteed-correct deterministic Urdu template - the same defense-in-depth pattern already used for crisis-language re-checking.

Backend, main.py

if language == "ur" and not is_urdu_script(result["message"]):
    return None # fall back to the verified-Urdu template

MMindHx20

05 / Urdu Language — Being Honest

A careful draft,
not a validated instrument —
and we say so.

The Urdu translation of PHQ-9, GAD-7, and K10 is a careful draft for this session, not a licensed clinical instrument - stated plainly on-screen whenever Urdu is active, not hidden in fine print. Item 9 (self-harm ideation) is safety-critical in the validated instrument; shipping an unlicensed translation as clinical-grade would be irresponsible.

The roadmap is explicit about this: partnering for a properly validated Urdu instrument is the single highest-leverage localization gap remaining - it would let MindHx move from a bilingual demo to a real screening tool usable in Urdu-speaking clinical settings.

07 / Technical Architecture

Two services.
Stateless by default.

A Next.js 16 (App Router) frontend and a FastAPI backend. The screening/risk-assessment endpoints are fully stateless - every request self-contained, nothing persisted. An optional layer adds PostgreSQL-backed accounts purely for people who choose to save history.

What's built —
and what's deliberately not.

  • Real acoustic biomarker vendor — today's signal is a heuristic proxy
  • Auth hardening — rate-limiting and httpOnly cookies still pending

08 / Future Possibilities

Where this goes
from here.

  • A validated Urdu instrument - partnering to properly validate the PHQ-9/GAD-7/K10 Urdu translation would unlock real clinical deployment in Urdu-speaking settings, not just a bilingual demo.
  • Score calibration - once labeled outcome data exists, Platt scaling or isotonic regression would let the score become an actual probability.
  • A real acoustic biomarker vendor - upgrading the voice signal from a heuristic proxy to a validated one.
  • Deeper Alibaba Cloud integration - Qwen/DashScope is already live; Function Compute and OSS-backed caching are natural next steps.
  • More regional languages - the same bilingual architecture (per-page copy objects, RTL-aware layout) generalizes to Punjabi, Pashto, or Sindhi.

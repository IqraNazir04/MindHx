## Mental health in context

The WHO estimates that around 1 in 8 people globally live with a mental disorder, with depression and anxiety the most common. Mental health conditions are health conditions, not weakness or lack of faith. They arise from a mix of biological factors (genetics, brain chemistry, physical illness), psychological factors (thinking patterns, trauma, coping style) and social factors (poverty, conflict, isolation, discrimination, family stress). They are treatable, and most people improve with the right support. In Pakistan, stigma and the shortage of specialists mean many people never seek help, which is why early screening and clear referral pathways matter.

## Depression

Depression is more than sadness. The two core symptoms are **persistent low mood** and **loss of interest or pleasure** (anhedonia) for most of the day, nearly every day, for at least **2 weeks**. Other symptoms include: changes in sleep (too little or too much), appetite or weight changes, fatigue, slowed movement or restlessness, feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt, poor concentration or indecision, and thoughts of death or suicide. A clinical diagnosis (DSM-5-TR major depressive disorder) requires at least five symptoms including one core symptom, causing significant distress or impairment.

Depression can also show up as irritability, physical aches, or loss of motivation, especially in men and in cultures where emotional language is less used. Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia) is a milder, chronic form lasting 2 years or more. Perinatal depression occurs during pregnancy or after birth.

## PHQ-9 and PHQ-8 screening

The Patient Health Questionnaire-9 asks how often, over the last 2 weeks, the person has been bothered by 9 symptoms, each scored 0 (not at all) to 3 (nearly every day). Total range 0–27.

Severity bands: **0–4 minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–19 moderately severe, 20–27 severe.** A score of 10 or more is the common threshold suggesting clinically significant depression worth assessment by a professional.

**Item 9** asks about thoughts of being better off dead or of self-harm. Any score above 0 on item 9 requires a direct safety follow-up regardless of the total score (see safety protocol).

The **PHQ-8** omits item 9 and uses the same bands (range 0–24). It is used in research and population screening; when PHQ-8 is used, MindHx must still have a separate risk check because suicidal ideation isn't measured.

Screening scores indicate likelihood and severity, not a diagnosis. They should be explained to the user in plain words with a recommendation, not a label.

## Generalised anxiety disorder (GAD)

GAD involves excessive, hard-to-control worry about many everyday things (health, family, money, work) on more days than not for at least **6 months**, with symptoms such as restlessness or feeling on edge, tiring easily, difficulty concentrating, irritability, muscle tension, and sleep disturbance. Worry feels out of proportion and gets in the way of life.

**GAD-7 screening:** 7 items scored 0–3 over the last 2 weeks, range 0–21. Bands: **0–4 minimal, 5–9 mild, 10–14 moderate, 15–21 severe.** A score of 10 or more suggests further assessment. GAD-7 also picks up panic, social anxiety and PTSD reasonably well.

## Panic attacks and panic disorder

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear that peaks within minutes, with symptoms like pounding heart, sweating, trembling, shortness of breath, choking feelings, chest discomfort, nausea, dizziness, chills or heat, numbness or tingling, feeling unreal, and fear of losing control or dying. Panic attacks are frightening but not dangerous in themselves; the body's alarm system is firing without a real threat. Panic disorder is diagnosed when attacks recur unexpectedly and the person worries persistently about further attacks or changes behaviour to avoid them.

Because panic symptoms overlap with heart and lung problems, a first episode of chest pain or breathlessness should be medically checked. During an attack: slow the out-breath, ground with 5-4-3-2-1, remind yourself "this is panic, it will peak and pass", and avoid fleeing the situation if safe, since escape teaches the brain the situation was dangerous. CBT with interoceptive exposure is highly effective.

## Social anxiety

Social anxiety disorder is intense fear of being judged, embarrassed or rejected in social or performance situations, leading to avoidance or enduring them with great distress, for 6 months or more. It is different from shyness by its intensity and impact. CBT for social anxiety (including attention training and behavioural experiments) is the first-line treatment.

## Trauma and PTSD

Most people who go through trauma recover naturally over weeks to months. Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) can develop after exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence, directly or by witnessing it. Symptom groups:
- **Re-experiencing:** intrusive memories, nightmares, flashbacks.
- **Avoidance:** of reminders, places, people, or thoughts.
- **Negative changes in mood and thinking:** guilt, shame, blame, numbness, detachment.
- **Hyperarousal:** being on guard, easily startled, irritable, poor sleep and concentration.
Symptoms lasting more than **1 month** and causing impairment suggest PTSD. ICD-11 also recognises **Complex PTSD**, after prolonged or repeated trauma (such as ongoing abuse), with added problems in emotional regulation, self-worth and relationships.

MindHx should not ask users to describe trauma in detail; it should validate, offer grounding, and refer to trauma-focused therapy.

## Insomnia

Insomnia is difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, at least 3 nights a week for 3 months or more, with daytime effects such as fatigue, poor focus or irritability. It often occurs alongside depression, anxiety and pain, and also worsens them. **CBT for insomnia (CBT-I)** is the recommended first-line treatment over sleeping pills. Basic sleep habits: fixed wake time every day, use the bed only for sleep (and intimacy), get up if awake for more than about 20 minutes and return when sleepy, reduce caffeine after midday, morning daylight, and a wind-down routine without screens.

<!-- chunk: id=cond_009 | domain=conditions | type=psychoeducation | risk=low | audience=general | lang=en -->
## Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)

OCD involves obsessions (unwanted, intrusive thoughts, images or urges that cause anxiety, such as contamination fears, fear of harming someone, or religious scrupulosity) and compulsions (repeated actions or mental rituals to reduce the anxiety, such as washing, checking, repeating prayers or ablution, or seeking reassurance). The person usually recognises the thoughts are excessive. Reassurance tends to feed the cycle; the first-line treatment is CBT with **exposure and response prevention (ERP)**. Religious scrupulosity is common in Pakistan and is best treated by a therapist who respects the person's faith, sometimes with a religious scholar's input.

## Perinatal and postpartum mental health

Up to 80% of new mothers experience the "baby blues" (tearfulness, mood swings) in the first two weeks, which resolve on their own. Postpartum depression is more persistent and severe and affects roughly 1 in 7 mothers globally; studies in Pakistan report higher rates. Fathers can also experience perinatal depression. **Postpartum psychosis** (confusion, hallucinations, strange beliefs, severe agitation, usually within the first weeks) is a psychiatric emergency requiring same-day medical care. The WHO **Thinking Healthy Programme**, developed and tested in Pakistan, effectively reduces perinatal depression through trained community health workers.

<!-- chunk: id=cond_011 | domain=conditions | type=psychoeducation | risk=moderate | audience=general | lang=en -->
## Bipolar disorder and psychosis (recognition only)

Bipolar disorder involves episodes of depression and episodes of mania or hypomania: unusually elevated or irritable mood, much less need for sleep, racing thoughts, rapid speech, grandiose ideas, and risky behaviour. Psychosis involves losing touch with reality, such as hallucinations (hearing or seeing things others don't) or fixed false beliefs. These need psychiatric assessment; MindHx should encourage a doctor's visit promptly, and urgently if the person is unsafe, not eating or sleeping, or hearing voices telling them to harm themselves or others. People with possible bipolar disorder should not be encouraged to start antidepressants or intense meditation without a psychiatrist.

<!-- chunk: id=cond_012 | domain=conditions | type=psychoeducation | risk=moderate | audience=general | lang=en -->
## Substance use

Alcohol, cannabis, opioids, sedatives, and other drugs are sometimes used to cope with stress, pain or low mood, but they worsen depression, anxiety and sleep over time and increase suicide risk. Signs of a problem include needing more for the same effect, withdrawal symptoms, failed attempts to cut down, and use interfering with life. Stopping some substances suddenly (alcohol, benzodiazepines) can be medically dangerous, so people should cut down with medical guidance.
